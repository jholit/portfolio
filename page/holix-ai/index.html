HOLIX Ai: Independent Product Design Project

HOLIX Ai is an ongoing independent Product Design project exploring how new AI capabilities can be integrated without making the product harder to understand or use.

Design question: When should a new capability be integrated into a product, and what structure does it need to justify its place?

My role: Product strategy, UX/UI design, information architecture, interaction design, prototyping, and front-end implementation using AI-assisted workflows.

Scope: The AI model itself is simulated. The project focuses on the product systems, workflows, controls, and interactions surrounding it.

HOLIX Ai

HOLIX Ai is an ongoing independent Product Design project exploring how an AI workspace can grow in capability while remaining understandable, organized, and under meaningful user control.

This began with frustrations Jack encountered while using existing AI products, particularly as new features, controls, and capabilities made once-simple experiences increasingly difficult to navigate and understand.

Rather than simply removing functionality, HOLIX Ai asks a different question: how can that capability be structured so people can understand what is available, where it belongs, and how it affects their experience?

That question continues to guide the project as new ideas and capabilities are introduced.

How does that thinking show up in the actual product?

HOLIX Ai

The workspace is organized around connected systems for navigation, conversation management, prompting, AI controls, and external tools rather than treating each new capability as an isolated feature.

The navigation gives conversations and workspace actions a clear hierarchy. The prompting area keeps relevant controls close to where users communicate with the AI, while contextual menus and progressive disclosure keep additional options available without exposing everything at once.

The HOLIX Hub extends that thinking to connected apps and services. Instead of scattering integrations throughout the interface, it provides one place to find, connect, filter, and manage them while keeping those capabilities connected to the broader workspace.

These decisions are intended to make the product easier to understand without removing the complexity required to make it capable.

The trade-off is deliberate: some advanced options require an extra moment of discovery in exchange for lower cognitive load, clearer hierarchy, and a more stable mental model. New capability does not automatically earn a new control or destination simply because it exists.

HOLIX Ai

HOLIX Ai is not a one-off set of finished screens. As the concept develops, new requirements have to fit decisions and structures that already exist.

Adding capabilities can expose weaknesses in earlier hierarchy, navigation, interactions, or controls. When that happens, he evaluates how the new requirement affects the surrounding experience and adjusts the product structure rather than simply adding another feature on top.

The project tests whether capability can grow without forcing people to relearn the product every time something new is introduced. Each addition has to fit the existing mental model, strengthen a real workflow, or justify a structural change.

The working prototype makes those decisions tangible through functional navigation, chat management, prompting and model controls, menus, the HOLIX Hub, responsive behavior, and represented interaction and accessibility states.

The AI itself is simulated because model development is outside the project's scope. The product surrounding the AI is what is being designed, tested, and developed.

Why This Matters

Capability alone isn’t justification. A feature earns its place when it strengthens the user’s mental model, supports a real workflow, and fits the surrounding product system. HOLIX Ai turns that principle into a working prototype, asking: are we serving the user, or the product?
